# Existing vs Proposed System

## Existing Cold-Storage Approaches

Conventional cold-storage systems generally depend on mechanical
refrigeration to maintain the required storage temperature. They can
provide controlled storage conditions, but their operation may require
continuous electrical energy and suitable infrastructure.

Solar-powered cold-storage systems can reduce dependence on grid
electricity by using solar PV generation and battery storage.

## Proposed System

The proposed Climate-Adaptive Solar Smart Mini Cold Storage combines
solar power with passive cooling, thermal insulation and intelligent
temperature-based cooling control.

The main difference is that active cooling is not treated as the only
method of temperature management. The system first utilizes insulation
and passive cooling and activates active cooling when the internal
temperature exceeds the defined threshold.

## Comparison

| Feature | Conventional Cold Storage | Proposed System |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Power | Grid electricity | Solar PV + Battery |
| Cooling Approach | Mainly active refrigeration | Passive cooling + active cooling when required |
| Energy Management | Continuous/controlled refrigeration | Temperature-based cooling operation |
| Grid Dependence | Generally higher | Reduced |
| Battery Backup | Not necessarily included | Included |
| Smart Control | Thermostat/control system | ESP32-based control |
| Environmental Monitoring | Temperature monitoring | Temperature + humidity monitoring |
| Storage Design | Conventional chamber | Insulated multi-compartment chamber |
| Deployment | Larger centralized facilities | Mini/decentralized farm-level concept |
| Scalability | Depends on facility design | Modular expansion can be considered |

## Research Basis

Post-harvest management literature identifies refrigerated storage,
temperature control and suitable relative humidity as important factors
in maintaining the quality and storage life of fresh produce.

ICAR also identifies post-harvest management and value addition as
important areas for reducing losses in horticultural crops.

The proposed system builds on these established principles while
combining them with solar energy and smart control.
